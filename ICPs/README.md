# PlayCourt ICP Personas — Overview

> **Purpose:** These Ideal Customer Profiles drive all content, messaging, positioning, and sales decisions for PlayCourt. Every piece of content should be written for at least one of these personas. Every feature priority should reference at least one of these pain points.

**Research conducted:** February 2026  
**Sources:** Reddit (r/10s, r/tennis), competitor review platforms (Capterra, Software Advice, GetApp), industry sources (NRPA, USTA, KK&W Club Consulting), competitor marketing (CourtReserve, EZFacility, ClubSpark, Cobalt, Club Automation), government parks & rec resources (GovPilot, CivicPlus)

## 🔑 Cross-Persona Insights

### What ALL three personas have in common:
1. **Mobile-first expectation** — Their end users (members/residents) are on phones. Clunky mobile experience = product failure.
2. **"Just works" reliability bar** — Every persona has been burned by software that breaks or behaves inconsistently. Reliability is table stakes.
3. **Support availability** — All three need to reach a real human when something goes wrong. Ticket-only support is a dealbreaker.
4. **Double-booking prevention** — Universally cited as a basic requirement. If the system allows double-booking, it fails.
5. **Admin reduction** — All three are time-constrained. The core value proposition is "spend less time on admin."

### What DIFFERENTIATES them:
- **Price sensitivity:** Persona 1 >> Persona 3 >> Persona 2 (Directors care more about fit than cost)
- **Tech sophistication:** Persona 2 >> Persona 3 >> Persona 1 (Directors are power users; volunteer managers are not)
- **Decision speed:** Persona 1 fast, Persona 2 medium, Persona 3 slow
- **Vocabulary:** Members (1,2) vs. Residents/Participants (3); Features (1) vs. Experience (2) vs. Outcomes (3)

## 🏆 Competitor Landscape (What They're Evaluating Against)

| Competitor | Primary Segment | Main Weaknesses (from research) |
|---|---|---|
| **CourtReserve** | Persona 1 & 2 | Pricing ($99–$499/month) feels high for small clubs; admin UI complexity |
| **EZFacility** | Persona 2 & 3 | "Clunky," "brutally slow," requires "multiple days of training"; dated interface |
| **ClubSpark** | Persona 1 & 2 | Limited marketing/automation; lacks modern UX |
| **Club Automation** | Persona 2 | Enterprise-only focus; steep learning curve; hidden fees reported |
| **Cobalt** | Persona 2 | Private club focused; not relevant for Personas 1 or 3 |
| **RecDesk / CivicPlus / ActiveNet** | Persona 3 | Designed for government but citizen-facing experience is poor; legacy tech |
| **Amilia** | Persona 3 | Municipality-focused but less tennis-specific |
| **Google Calendar / Spreadsheets** | Persona 1 | Free but zero booking management; Persona 1's starting point |

**PlayCourt's opportunity:** There is a clear gap between the complexity/cost of enterprise tools (CourtReserve, Club Automation) and the total inadequacy of DIY tools (Google Calendar). Small clubs and municipal coordinators especially lack a modern, simple, affordable option.

## 🛠️ How to Use These Personas

### For Content Writers (Writer Agent):
- Each persona file has a "Writer Agent Notes" section at the bottom with tone, vocabulary, CTAs, and format guidance.
- Always identify the primary persona before writing. If multi-persona, note the primary and the secondary.
- Use the persona's own language — especially their quotes. Mirror it back in content.

### For Product:
- Pain points sections are directly actionable as feature requirements.
- Objection sections reveal UX friction to address (setup complexity, mobile UX, data export).
- Decision factors sections are the feature priority hierarchy per segment.

### For Sales:
- Decision factors = what to lead with in discovery calls.
- Trigger events = qualifying questions ("What made you start looking now?").
- Objections = anticipate and address in demo.
- Language & tone = how to communicate in outreach.
