## 🌅 Day in the Life

**6:30 AM** – Arrives before courts open. Reviews the day's court sheet. Four private lessons, two clinics, a league match in the afternoon. Checks who's on court at 7am.

**7:00 AM** – Starts teaching. Also fielding texts from members who want to reschedule their Thursday lesson.

**10:00 AM** – Clinic wraps. Quick check of reservation system — sees a double booking that staff created manually. Spends 20 minutes sorting it out. Notes that members are calling the desk to book instead of using the app.

**11:00 AM** – Meeting with GM about the quarterly member satisfaction survey. Two complaints about court availability during peak hours. Need to show data on utilization to defend current booking rules.

**1:00 PM** – Team meeting with assistant pros. Reviews next month's programming schedule — new adult clinic series to plan. Current software requires creating each date manually instead of recurring series.

**3:00 PM** – Two members stop by to request their "regular Thursday 4pm" back. They held it last year but the new booking system doesn't support holds. Director has to manually work it out.

**4:00 PM** – League match starts. Coordinator handles it but Director checks in to make sure it's running smooth.

**6:00 PM** – Evening clinic. Most popular time slot. Waitlist isn't being managed well — members are emailing to be added, someone missed the spot because they weren't notified promptly.

**8:00 PM** – Home. Checks email. Three members want confirmation for their weekend doubles reservation.

## 😤 Pain Points (Ranked by Intensity)

### #1 — Member Friction with Booking System
Members (especially older ones) find the booking portal confusing or inconvenient. They resort to calling the pro shop, emailing the director, or texting staff — undermining the whole point of having software. Member complaints about the booking experience get escalated to the GM.

*"If my members can't figure out how to book a court, they blame me. Not the software."*

### #2 — Juggling Multiple Disconnected Tools
Court reservations in one system, lesson booking elsewhere, program registration in another, communications via email blast, financials in the club's main POS — nothing talks to each other. Staff are re-entering data. Reports require manual assembly.

*"I need three different logins just to answer the question 'who's on court 4 at 3pm?'"*

### #3 — No Real Data on Member Engagement / Court Utilization
The director needs to defend programming decisions to the GM and tennis committee. But pulling court utilization data, revenue per lesson, member activity rates, or clinic fill rates requires manual report generation that's time-consuming and error-prone.

### #4 — Lesson & Program Scheduling is a Black Box to Members
Members don't know what clinics are available, when spots open up, or how to sign up without calling. Waitlist management is manual. Members who want more tennis feel the club doesn't offer it — when actually it does, they just can't find it.

### #5 — Staff Coordination and Accountability
Multiple pros teaching, covering for each other, sharing courts — without a unified system, coverage gaps happen. Schedule changes aren't communicated to members who booked. Manual coordination via text/WhatsApp is unprofessional and creates miscommunication.

## 🎯 Goals (What Success Looks Like)

1. **Frictionless member booking experience** — Members book courts, lessons, and clinics from one app, from their phone, without calling the pro shop
2. **Program fill rates** — Clinics and leagues fill quickly because discovery and registration are easy
3. **Data for GM conversations** — Can pull utilization reports, revenue data, and member activity reports in minutes
4. **Staff efficiency** — All scheduling in one system; pros know their schedule without coordinating via personal phones
5. **Member retention through engagement** — Members who play more, stay longer; software helps drive participation
6. **Showcase the tennis program** — At member events, on club app, the tennis program looks as premium as the rest of the club

---

## 🔍 Decision Factors (Ranked)

1. **Member-Facing Experience** — If the member-facing app/portal is clunky, it doesn't matter how good the backend is. This is #1.
2. **Integration with Club's Existing Systems** — Must work with (or at least not conflict with) the club's main billing/POS system
3. **Features** — Court scheduling, lesson booking, program management, waitlist, communications — all must be strong
4. **Data & Reporting** — Can they pull the reports GM/committee will ask for?
5. **Staff Usability** — Pros and desk staff need to be able to use it without intensive training
6. **Price** — Less price-sensitive than other personas; $300–$600/month is acceptable if features justify it; enterprise pricing for large clubs
7. **Support & Onboarding** — Migration from old system is critical; needs white-glove onboarding
8. **Brand/Prestige** — Matters more here than other segments; "does this look premium?"

## 🚧 Objections (Why They Hesitate)

| Objection | Underlying Fear |
|-----------|----------------|
| "We just switched software 2 years ago" | Migration pain; internal political capital spent; don't want to do it again |
| "Our GM would have to approve this" | Can't make this call unilaterally; needs to build internal business case |
| "Will this integrate with our Jonas/Clubessential system?" | Afraid of creating data silos or duplicate billing |
| "Our members are older — they won't adopt it" | Past experience with adoption failures; fear of member backlash |
| "The onboarding looks complicated" | CourtReserve and others have notoriously steep setup curves |
| "How do we handle data migration from [current system]?" | Don't want to lose historical booking data, member profiles, lesson records |
| "We already use [ClubSpark/CourtReserve] — what makes you different?" | Need clear, honest differentiation without overselling |

## 🗣️ Language & Tone

**How they speak:**
- Professional, but passionate about tennis
- Uses industry terms: "court sheet," "drill night," "clinics," "UTR," "USTA league," "house accounts," "member-guest"
- Results-oriented in GM conversations: "court utilization," "revenue per court hour," "member retention"
- Peer-to-peer: more casual; "what are you guys using for reservations?"
- Proud of their programming; frustrated when software makes it look bad

**Technical level:** Moderate-to-high. Has used multiple platforms. Knows what features should exist. Can evaluate software demos critically. Has probably hit the limitations of CourtReserve, EZFacility, or Club Automation.

**Emotional register:** Ambitious and performance-oriented. They want their program to be excellent. Software is a tool to that end — they'll invest if it delivers.

7. **New club opening or expansion** — Adding courts; current system can't scale

## ✍️ Writer Agent Notes — Content Adaptation

**When writing for this persona:**

- **Lead with member experience and program prestige** — "Your members deserve better than calling the pro shop to book a court"
- **Use business language alongside tennis language** — blend "court utilization" with "member retention" and "program revenue"
- **Show integration story** — Address the "does it work with our existing systems?" question upfront
- **Reference the GM conversation** — Content that helps them make the internal case is valuable ("here's how to present this to your GM")
- **Competitor comparison is welcome** — They've evaluated tools before; don't shy away from honest comparison with CourtReserve, EZFacility, Club Automation, Cobalt
- **Data and reporting story** — Show screenshots or examples of the kind of reports they can pull
- **White-glove onboarding** — Emphasize dedicated onboarding support, data migration assistance, staff training
- **Prestige matters** — The platform should look and feel premium; mention that members will see a professional booking experience

**Tone:** Professional, peer-to-peer. Like a colleague in the industry who gets what it's like to run a club program. Respect their expertise.

**CTA that works:** "Book a 30-minute demo" or "See how [Club Name] improved utilization by X%"

**CTA that doesn't work:** "Try free" (feels too small-scale for their context)

**Format preferences:** Long-form case studies, detailed comparison tables, ROI calculators for GM presentations
