# ICP Persona 1: The Small Club Manager
*"I just need something that works and doesn't cost a fortune."*

---

---

## 👤 Demographics

- **Age range:** 38–58 years old
- **Education:** Varies widely — some have sports management backgrounds, most transitioned from other careers and volunteer/took on the role
- **Income/Background:** Middle class; the management role is often unpaid or minimally compensated
- **How they got here:** Former competitive player, long-time club member, or parent who got roped in; often "the most organized person who showed up to the AGM"
- **Time in role:** 1–8 years; high turnover as volunteers burn out
- **Reports to:** Club board or homeowners association board
- **Influences decisions:** Themselves + 3–5 board members (often including president and treasurer)

---

## 🌅 Day in the Life

**6:30 AM** – Checks phone; three texts from members asking about court availability for this weekend. Responds individually.

**8:00 AM** – Logs into Google Sheet to check who has reserved what. Someone double-booked Court 3.

**9:00 AM** – Processes two new membership applications via email — copies data into a spreadsheet, sends invoice via PayPal or Venmo.

**10:30 AM** – League night is Thursday. Sends reminder email via Gmail to a list manually updated last season. Wonders who's dropped out.

**12:00 PM** – Gets a call from a member who "didn't know" another group had booked their usual court time.

**2:00 PM** – Spends 45 minutes figuring out court utilization for the board meeting next week — pulling from multiple tabs.

**5:00 PM** – Updates Facebook Group with this week's schedule. Half the club isn't in the group.

**Evening** – Answers three more "is [time] available?" messages. Considers quitting the role.

---

## 😤 Pain Points (Ranked by Intensity)

### #1 — Manual Booking = Constant Admin Chaos
The current system (Google Calendar, spreadsheets, phone calls, Facebook posts) creates a constant stream of messages and conflicts. Double-bookings happen. Members get frustrated. The coordinator gets blamed. **Every booking is a phone call or text they have to personally handle.**

*"I spend more time managing texts about court time than I do actually playing tennis."*

### #2 — No Easy Way to Collect Membership Fees
Members pay by check, Venmo, e-transfer — whatever's easiest for them. Tracking who has paid is a manual nightmare. Sending reminders is awkward and time-consuming. Chasing dues feels like a personal confrontation.

*"I'm basically a volunteer debt collector at this point."*

### #3 — Members Who Hoard Prime Slots
A recurring problem: a few members create recurring bookings or always grab peak evening slots, while others can't get on the courts. No system to enforce fairness rules (max booking limits, advance booking windows).

### #4 — Communication Gaps
Different members use different channels — Facebook, email, text, WhatsApp. There's no single source of truth for schedules, cancellations, or announcements. Every update requires posting in 3 places.

### #5 — No Data / No Reporting
When the board asks "how many members do we have?" or "which courts are most used?" — the coordinator has to manually count spreadsheet rows. They have no visibility into utilization, revenue trends, or membership health.

---

## 🎯 Goals (What Success Looks Like)

1. **Reduce interruptions** — Members self-serve; coordinator isn't bombarded with texts
2. **Membership dues on autopilot** — Auto-reminders, online payment, tracking in one place
3. **Fair court access** — Configurable rules that prevent slot hoarding without confrontation
4. **One communication channel** — All members on same platform, getting same info
5. **Stay within board-approved budget** — Justify the cost to skeptical treasurer
6. **Keep the role sustainable** — Hand off cleanly when they step down; successor can figure it out

---

## 🔍 Decision Factors (Ranked)

1. **Price** — This is #1. Must fit a club budget of $50–$200/month. Any hint of "call for pricing" is a red flag. They want to see pricing upfront.
2. **Ease of Setup** — If it takes more than a few hours to set up, they'll never finish. They don't have IT support.
3. **Member Ease of Use** — If members complain it's confusing, it's a failure regardless of features. Their members include 70-year-old retirees.
4. **Mobile-Friendly** — Members book from phones; must work on mobile without friction.
5. **Support** — They need real humans to answer questions, not a ticketing system.
6. **Features** — Nice to have; they'll sacrifice features for simplicity and price.

## 🚧 Objections (Why They Hesitate)

| Objection | Underlying Fear |
|-----------|----------------|
| "Our members won't use an app" | Risk of adoption failure; embarrassment in front of board |
| "This seems too complicated to set up" | They tried CourtReserve or EZFacility and got overwhelmed |
| "We can't afford software" | Board treasurer will veto it; need to justify ROI |
| "What happens to our data if we cancel?" | Fear of lock-in after hearing horror stories |
| "We already use Google Calendar, it's fine" | Status quo bias; change feels like extra work |
| "We only use it seasonally — paying year-round?" | Seasonal clubs feel full-year SaaS is wasteful |

## 🗣️ Language & Tone

**How they speak:**
- Casual, practical, self-deprecating humor about the chaos
- "I just need it to be simple"
- Sports-adjacent vocabulary ("court time," "drill night," "ladder," "house league")
- Not technical — don't say "API integration" or "workflow automation"
- Do say: "set it and forget it," "members can book themselves," "no more texts at 11pm"

**Technical level:** Low-to-moderate. Comfortable with phones and basic web apps but intimidated by anything that requires configuration or IT thinking.

**Emotional register:** Frustrated but optimistic. They care about their club. They want to do a good job. They're just tired of doing it manually.

---

## ⚡ Trigger Events (What Makes Them Search NOW)

1. **Big conflict/complaint** — A double-booking or slot-hoarding incident blew up at the AGM
2. **Volunteer burnout** — Previous coordinator quit; new person doesn't want to inherit chaos
3. **Membership growth** — Club added courts or members; current system is visibly breaking
4. **Board mandate** — Board said "modernize the booking system" at the last meeting
5. **Nearby club switched** — They heard Club XYZ in the next town now uses an app and members love it
6. **Season start** — April/May panic when spring season is about to start with no system ready

## ✍️ Writer Agent Notes — Content Adaptation

**When writing for this persona:**

- **Lead with simplicity and price** — don't bury it. They want to see "$X/month" immediately.
- **Show the relief, not the features** — "No more booking texts at 11pm" beats "automated reservation management"
- **Use before/after framing** — "Before: 30 texts a day. After: members book themselves."
- **Social proof from clubs their size** — Quote a 200-member community club, not a 1,000-member private club
- **Keep it short** — They're time-poor volunteers. Long emails/pages get abandoned.
- **Address the board** — They have to sell this internally. Give them a one-liner for the treasurer.
- **Avoid jargon** — No "SaaS," "workflow," "API," "onboarding." Use plain English.
- **Acknowledge seasonal clubs** — If applicable, mention seasonal pricing or month-to-month flexibility.

**Tone:** Warm, practical, slightly humorous about the chaos. Like a helpful fellow club manager sharing a tip at the tennis social.

**CTA that works:** "Try free for 30 days, no card required" or "Set up your club in under an hour"

**CTA that doesn't work:** "Book a demo" (feels corporate, slow, high-commitment)
